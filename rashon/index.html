The One Move

The post that blew up was a teaching post wearing a flex costume.

“When Ye says just play the chords… you PLAY the chords.” The overlay says Just Play The Chords. The frame is your rig. A million people watched a man play chords, and eleven thousand of them typed something.

The thing they typed

“What chords?”

You never answered. There was nothing to answer with. Build the answer, and make it the thing they get instantly.

  • →The magnet: “The chords from that Ye video.” Two-minute video or a one-page chart, delivered the second they give an email. Not a newsletter. Not “updates about.”
  • →One field. Email. First and last name are two extra decisions you don’t need and can ask for later.
  • →The distribution is already built. Eleven thousand comments are a permanent, searchable, warm audience. Reply to them. Pin the link. Repost the reel with the answer as the follow-up.
  • →It costs you an afternoon — and it is the entire difference between a viral moment and a business.

The Format

Keys POV, without needing a stadium.

Your signature already exists — it’s on the viral thumbnail and half your grid. What it needs is a version you can shoot in your own room. Six of your last nine posts were “look where I am” tour documentation, which is a format with a dependency you don’t control: when the tour pauses, the posting stops. Your cadence went from weekly in May to one post every nine days by August for exactly that reason.

The repeatable unit: one famous song → the one chord move that makes it work → played on the Nord → thirty seconds → the hook is the question, not the credential. Twice a week, same frame.

The Order

Seven moves, ranked by leverage per hour.

Not by size. The first three are yours and they’re small; the rest are the machinery that makes the first three keep paying.

  1. 10 min
    Fix the three links.

    Nothing downstream works until traffic can actually route to you. This is the cheapest thing on the page and it has been broken through a million-like reel.

  2. Afternoon
    Ship the chords magnet.

    One video, one email field, instant delivery. Converts eleven thousand warm comments into a list you own rather than rent.

  3. 1 hour
    Answer the viral post in public.

    Reply in the comments, pin the link, repost the reel with the answer. That audience is still there and it cost nothing to reach the first time.

  4. Ongoing
    Two Keys-POV posts a week, one format.

    Breaks the tour dependency. Consistency of format is what compounds on Instagram — not volume, and not variety.

  5. Weeks
    Start YouTube.

    You don’t have a channel. For someone selling piano teaching that is the single largest structural gap here. Shorts are the cuts you’re already making; long-form is where the course actually funnels.

  6. 1 hour
    Put a price and a calendar on the lessons page.

    Revenue that needs no course to exist. You’re already selling this; you’re just not letting anyone buy it.

  7. 1 hour
    Pick one sentence. Retire the dead Facebook Page.

    Your site, Instagram, LinkedIn and Jammcard each describe a different person, and there are two Facebook pages — one with 5.1K followers, one with 97 and nothing since February.

Honesty

What this read couldn’t see.

Everything above came from your public pages. That’s a real limit and it’s worth stating plainly, because a confident wrong number is worse than a missing one.

Only twelve posts are visible without logging in

Your full archive isn’t measured here. The cadence figure covers 15 May to 3 August only — nine posts, which is the complete visible run in that window but not your whole year.

No views, saves, shares or reach — so no true engagement rate

Logged-out Instagram gives likes and comments and nothing else. Every ratio on this page is measured against follower count, which is a blunter instrument than the real thing. One look at your own Instagram Insights would settle several questions this page can only frame — particularly whether the cadence drop is costing you reach or whether format is the whole story.

The “~19K in April” figure is second-hand

It comes from an April record whose own source isn’t stated. The 25.5K is a live read today. So the growth figure is directional, not measured — treat “under 1% converted” as the right order of magnitude rather than a precise number.

“1M likes” is Instagram’s rounded display

Not an exact count. Same for the 11K comments and the 116K on the January post. They’re Instagram’s own figures, read from each post, but they’re rounded at that scale.

Your list size, site analytics and DMs are invisible from outside

It’s possible you captured meaningfully more than assumed here. The structure of the signup page argues against it — but that’s an argument, not proof, and you have the actual number.

Whether you still want a course at all

This whole page assumes the goal from earlier this year is still live. Plenty has changed since — you have production credits on BULLY now, including the Lauryn Hill record, and you’re touring arenas in Asia. If the answer is that the teaching business is no longer the point, most of this page is the wrong page, and that’s worth saying before anyone acts on it.
