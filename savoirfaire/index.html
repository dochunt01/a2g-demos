Side A · The engagements

What the night
actually looks like.

Most violinists sell you an hour. What you are buying is the way a room feels at a particular moment.

A1

Weddings

Prelude as guests are seated, the processional, the recessional, then straight on through the cocktail hour. Your song arranged for violin and recorded for you first, so nothing about the aisle is a surprise on the day.

A2

Corporate & galas

Receptions, openings, donor evenings. He arrives self-contained — system, stands, cabling — and plays the room rather than a setlist. Formal attire, on site forty-five minutes early, sound handled end to end.

A3

Private evenings

Anniversaries, dinners, memorials. Quieter and closer, often acoustic. This is where forty years of knowing when to play — and when to stop — earns its keep.

Side A · Track 01

The player.

Named for the cartoon mouse — an affectionate nod to a knack for finding ways for his musicians to earn enough to focus on the craft. It stuck.

On the job

He was three when he started playing at his parents' church, and five when he first walked onto the stage at Chicago's Orchestra Hall — one of a small group of Suzuki prodigies under Betty Haag, on a public television broadcast that became an annual event for over thirty-five years.

He studied at the Merit Music School: theory, the youth orchestras, private violin with Joseph Williams. At sixteen, conductor Frank Winkler invited him to lead the principal viola desk of the City Youth String ensemble. A Merit grant sent him to Interlochen for classical viola.

It was at Interlochen, sitting in at a jazz workshop with Milt Jackson, that the direction changed. Years later, working at Symphony Center, he played for Wynton Marsalis — who told him to go and study jazz violin properly. Johnny Frigo took him under his wing.

In 2000, the Chicago chapter of the AACM voted him in.

Engagements
& rates.

Solo violin for a ceremony. A duo through cocktails. The quartet when the room is large and the night is long. He will tell you which one your room needs — including when that is the cheaper answer.

A1

The wedding day

Ceremony, cocktail hour and reception, all in one space. Solo violin with his own sound. He is not counting your hours — he is holding the day.

$1,500Solo · one space
A2

Corporate & gala

Receptions, openings, donor evenings, holiday parties. Up to two hours on site, breaks included. Three hours $1,750, four hours $2,000.

$1,500Solo · to two hours
A3

A second space

A garden ceremony and a ballroom reception means a second sound system carried in and set up. Each additional space is its own setup.

+$500Per extra space
A4

A larger room

Past about 150 guests his own system is not enough. A larger rig, and an engineer to run it so he can play.

+$1,000Over 150 guests
One rule underneath all of itHe charges for a day and a setup, not for hours. That is why a second room costs the same whether the music there lasts twenty minutes or two hours — it is a second system carried in and set up. Half the total holds your date and is non-refundable; the balance is due the week of the event.

A QSC 1000-watt system covering a room of roughly a hundred without a house engineer. Stands, cabling, the lot.

Anywhere inside Chicagoland. Beyond that quoted honestly and separately — never buried in the rate.
