Posting rules
six rules, written for Facebook
  • Text carries the hook, not sound. Feed video plays muted; the winning post won on four lines of on-screen text.
  • State a memory, leave a gap. "Back in the day, when music felt like this" works because the viewer supplies the memory. A label gives them nothing to add.
  • Name one artist, never a genre — a name is a person people have a position about. (The lane-C manifesto is the deliberate exception: name six.)
  • Long captions are fine on Facebook. This audience reads. The opposite of Instagram.
  • The ask is a comment, never a link in the post. Details in the next fold.
  • Same frame every time. One camera spot, one overlay style — recognition before reading.
The comment-to-DM move
the one thing on every post

"Comment TAKE ME BACK and I'll send you the link." Four jobs at once:

  • Comments are what Facebook rewards — the ask becomes the algorithm's fuel.
  • It builds a list the band owns. The venue has the ticket buyers; Aniba gets everyone who raised a hand, in her own inbox, for the next show.
  • It's the only ticket attribution available when the checkout belongs to the venue.
  • A comment opens Meta's 24-hour window where a promotional DM is allowed — this is inside the rules and outside every invite cap.
Links
why the link never goes in the post

Meta is testing a cap of two link posts per month on creator pages, lifted only by a paid subscription (confirmed by Meta, December 2025; news publishers excluded — creator pages are the target). Links in comments and Stories are exempt, and so are links to other Facebook pages — which the event page is. By Meta's own report, 97.9% of what Americans see on Facebook has no outbound link at all.

So: the post carries the comment ask, the reply DM carries the Eventbrite link, Stories carry it too, and the event link can go anywhere freely.

The event page
the band owns this one

The Facebook event (facebook.com/events/1659670468471612) is hosted by the band, Neri Honey and Ishmael — not the venue. Every Interested tap buys a free reminder from Facebook the day before and again on show day. That's the machinery that fixes "meant to come, forgot."

  • Responses spread on their own — Interested/Going shows in friends' feeds, the right Chicago graph.
  • Each host can invite 500 people; sharing, Groups, "guests can invite friends," comment-DMs, and Event Response ads are all uncapped routes.
  • Facebook's own suggested-events panel put Jill Scott at The Chicago Theatre, Sunday, August 23, 2026 — 10,000+ interested — right under this event. Same city, same weekend, same music. The Friday show is the warm-up for a weekend those people already planned; that's a post angle and an ad audience, free.
Invite people who'd plausibly come, in batches over days — Facebook throttles accounts whose invites go unanswered. And never make a duplicate event; it splits the social proof.

Paid
what money can and can't do here

Final spend: $59.68 — Phase 1 only. The $220 second phase was cancelled on Wednesday, August 20, so the planned $300 budget was never spent. What ran, what it reached and who it reached is on its own page: The Boost Report. This fold keeps only the reasoning about what money can and can't do on this show.

Purchase-optimized ads can't work here — the ticket checkout is the venue's Eventbrite, only the organizer can add a tracking pixel, and that's Haven, not the band. No pixel means no purchase optimization and no conversion counts. Retargeting audiences are also out, for a different reason: building a custom audience takes a business Page with an ad account, and the winning posts live on Aniba's personal profile, which can boost but can't build audiences. Interest-plus-radius targeting is the strongest tool this surface has — and for a local show, it's enough.

Judge paid on comments, DMs and event responses — never on tickets, which can't be measured here. Organic stays the engine: a 4,400-view post is roughly $30 of media at 2026 awareness CPMs, landing on warmer people than any cold ad reaches. The money's job is reminding, not filling.

How much to post
revised August 14, 2026 — content type is the lever, not count

An earlier version of this section said a second daily post steals impressions from the first. That was withdrawn on August 14, 2026 — that rule comes from how the follower feed rations a Page's posts, and these are reels: Facebook scores each reel on its own with cold viewers, and the data agrees — the biggest post in the set landed the day after the second-biggest, back to back, with no penalty.

So why still one a day through Tuesday? Because frequency isn't what reach comes from. A nostalgia clip does 4,000+; show content does 650–900 no matter when it goes out. A second daily post would mostly show the same warm people the same pitch twice — effort that's better spent making the one post the right type.

Thursday and Friday, go to two a day on purpose. In the last 48 hours the job flips from reaching new people to reminding people who already decided — hitting the same audience twice is then the whole point.

"More" that costs nothing any day: Stories 3–5 a day (where the link lives) · replying to every comment (pushes the post back up) · six band members resharing (six networks, free) · the Groups above · an Instagram cross-post of the same reel (smaller audience, zero extra shooting).

TikTok
the long game — not this show

Audited August 10, 2026: 103 videos, 43,257 total views, median 291, best ever 1,963. TikTok seeds each video to a ~200–500 person test pool and expands only on strong completion — these are dying in the pool. The captions are Facebook captions (show recaps, inspirational quotes) shown to strangers who owe her nothing.

TikTok distributes by interest, not neighbourhood — it cannot sell tickets to a Chicago Friday. Park it until after the show, then: open on the singing, much shorter clips, seamless loops, display name changed to "Aniba & The Sol Starz — Chicago Neo Soul" (search reads that field), 3–5 hashtags used as search terms with the artist's name, and say the artist's name out loud — TikTok transcribes audio for search. The handle is @anibathesolstarz — note it differs from the Instagram handle, worth unifying someday.
