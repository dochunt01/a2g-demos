    WHY 8:45, NOT 9:00

    Land before the scroll, not during it

    Facebook needs a few minutes to push a new post out. Going up slightly early puts it in front of people as the 9PM wind-down starts instead of halfway through it.

    WHY THE SAME SLOT

    A time is part of the ritual

    Same hour every night is the scheduling version of “same frame every time.” People start expecting it, and Facebook learns who to show it to.

    WHY THREE PROBES

    Otherwise we learn nothing

    If all eight go up at 9PM we end these eleven days knowing exactly what we know now. Three posts at genuinely different hours is what turns the run into an answer.

    WHY FRIDAY & SATURDAY NIGHT

    The mindset matches the ask

    Someone scrolling at 9PM on a weekend is already thinking about going out. That's the frame of mind that buys a ticket to a Friday night show — which is the one benchmark charts can't see.

    Before any of this — check her own numbers. Meta Business Suite → Insights → Audience → the activity chart. It shows the hours her followers were actually online over the last 28 days, broken out by day. That's her real audience, not an average of 300,000 other pages. If that chart disagrees with anything in the table above, the chart wins and we rebuild the schedule around it. It takes about a minute to pull.
    One caveat on reading the result. The probes carry different artists than the anchors, so if midday underperforms we won't know for certain whether it was the hour or the name. With eleven posts there's no way around that. What comes out is a direction worth acting on, not proof — and it's still eleven days and zero dollars to get it.
    LinksKeep the link out of the post. +

    Not for the reason everyone repeats. The “link posts get 80% less reach” figure floating around traces back to marketing blogs, not to data, and it's shakier than it sounds — Facebook referral traffic to news sites actually rose sharply through 2026. The real reason is narrower and much more concrete.

    THE ACTUAL RULE

    Two links a month, or pay

    Meta is testing a hard cap of two link posts per month on professional accounts and Pages, lifted only by a Meta Verified subscription starting at $14.99/month. Confirmed by Meta in December 2025 as “a limited test.” News publishers are excluded. Creator pages are exactly who it targets.

    THE EXEMPTION

    Comments don't count

    Links posted in the comments are unlimited and unaffected. So is anything pointing at Instagram, WhatsApp, or another Facebook post.

    THE SCALE OF IT

    Links barely appear in feeds anyway

    By Meta's own reporting, 97.9% of what Americans actually saw on Facebook contained no outbound link at all — up from 86.5% three years earlier. Whatever the mechanism, the outcome is settled.

    SO

    Comment, then DM

    Which is what the plan already said. It now has a documented reason behind it instead of folklore — and it's the one route Meta is actively pushing people toward.

    PaidTicket ads can't work. Event ads can. +

    There's a specific reason paid traffic can't be optimized toward a ticket sale for August 21 — and one clean way around it that costs nothing to set up. Both come down to where the conversion happens.

    You can't put a pixel on this checkout. Eventbrite does support Meta pixels — the organizer pastes a pixel ID under Marketing → Tracking pixels, and it takes about a minute. But only the organizer can do it, and the organizer is Haven Entertainment Center. Without that pixel there's no way to tell Facebook what a sale looks like, no way to optimize toward one, and no way to retarget anyone who visited the ticket page and didn't buy.
    And the math doesn't clear either. A Meta ad set needs roughly 50 conversion events a week to stop guessing and start optimizing. The minimum daily budget to get there is target-cost-per-ticket × 50 ÷ 7 — so even at a generous $10 a ticket, that's about $71 a day, and not one of those sales could be counted without the pixel. A smaller budget doesn't run slower, it runs permanently unstable. Eleven days isn't two full learning cycles regardless.
    The way around it

    Event Response ads need no pixel at all

    Meta's Engagement objective has an Event Response setting that optimizes for people tapping Interested or Going on a Facebook event. That tap happens inside Facebook — so there's nothing to install, nothing on Eventbrite to touch, and no permission to ask anyone for. Meta's own documentation says it plainly: it works for an event with or without tickets and doesn't need the conversion tracking that Sales campaigns require.

    And an Interested tap isn't a vanity metric here — it triggers the reminder machinery described in the next section. This is the paid motion to run this week, if any budget runs at all. Measure the actual cost per response rather than trusting anyone's estimate; at this scale it's the only number worth knowing.

    What else money could do this week

    • Free

      One email. If they'll drop Aniba's pixel ID into the Eventbrite listing, real purchase tracking becomes possible — and it costs them five minutes. Worth knowing the answer before the next booking either way.

    • Best

    • Free

    • Read

      Not on tickets. Tickets aren't measurable here, and pretending otherwise just produces a confident number that means nothing.

    THE INSTINCT — KEEP IT

    Let organic pick the winner first

    Posting free is a test nobody charges for. Most people spend before they know what works; running eleven posts and funding only the ones that already earned attention is the correct order. That not every post hits 4,000 isn't a problem — it's the test doing its job.

    ADJUSTMENT 1

    Not to the same people

    A post that already reached 4,000 has mostly finished with the warm audience — whoever was going to see it, saw it. Spending to show it to followers again buys repetition, not reach. Point it at people who haven't seen it: a tight Chicago radius, neo-soul interests, and the Jill Scott audience below.

    ADJUSTMENT 2

    Not the Boost button

    Boost defaults to optimizing for likes and comments — the wrong currency here. Run the same winning video through Ads Manager as an Event Response campaign instead. Same money, and each response it buys picks up Facebook's free reminders for August 20th and show day.

    IF ONLY ONE THING GETS FUNDED

    Fund the event, not the post

    A boosted post stops working the minute the budget stops. An event response keeps working until August 21, because Facebook keeps reminding that person for free. The spend compounds instead of evaporating.

    The real lesson is the same one as the ticketing. Spending to fill a room you don't own, through a checkout you can't see, is how you spend money and learn nothing. The version of this worth doing properly is a show where the band holds the ticket link — then the pixel, the retargeting, and the numbers all become available at once.

    VolumeOrganic already beats the ad budget. Posting more won't extend that. +

    Two separate questions live in “should we post more” — is free traffic actually competitive with paid, and does doubling the posts double the reach. The first answer is yes. The second is no, and pushing it backfires.

    What the free posts are already worth. A post pulling ~4,000 views is delivering what would cost roughly $30 to buy — brand-awareness CPMs on Facebook average about $7.50 per thousand impressions in 2026. Across eleven days of daily posting that's around 44,000 impressions, or roughly $330 of media, for nothing. And it's landing on people who already follow her, which for a local show eleven days out is a warmer, likelier-to-buy audience than any budget could assemble from cold in the same window.
    But the ceiling is real, and it bites early. The measured sweet spot for a Facebook Page is 5–7 posts a week. Returns flatten above 10 a week and engagement falls off below 3. The mechanism is the part that matters: Facebook caps how much of any one Page it shows a given person, so a second post in a day mostly steals impressions from the first — same audience, split attention. Worse, more posts at a lower engagement rate teaches the algorithm the Page is less interesting, which pulls down the reach of everything that follows.

    Eleven posts across eleven days is exactly 7 a week. The plan is already at the top of the useful range. Adding a second daily post moves it into the zone where the data says it starts costing reach rather than buying it.

    The actual lever: post the same things as Reels

    Her posts are landing at roughly half to two-thirds of her follower count, which is the shape of feed distribution — it's capped by how many people follow her. Reels aren't. They're the one Facebook format that reaches non-followers at scale, they average 67% more reach than standard posts, and up to half of what people see in Feed now comes from accounts they don't follow. Same footage, same caption, same 8:45 slot — published as a Reel instead. That raises the ceiling without adding a single post.

    So hashtags aren't the problem

    The fixes, in order of what they're worth

    FIX 01 · THE WHOLE GAME

    Open on the singing

    No talking intro, no title card, no “hey y'all.” Her voice in the first frame. Completion is the heaviest ranking signal there is, and everything before the vocal is a reason to swipe.

    FIX 02

    Much shorter than feels right

    A 20-second clip watched to 80% beats a 60-second clip watched to 40% — TikTok scores the percentage, not the seconds. Post the one bar people would rewind, not the song.

    FIX 03

    Make it loop

    End on the note it started on so the replay is seamless. A loop that catches someone twice counts as completion twice, and it's the cheapest trick on the platform.

    FIX 04 · FREE, 10 SECONDS

    Fix the name field

    The display name is currently the raw handle, “anibathesolstarz.” TikTok's search indexes that field. Change it to Aniba & The Sol Starz — Chicago Neo Soul and the account becomes findable by people searching those words.

    The Session — film the room properly

    The highest-leverage change available. Every independent artist who broke through in the last decade turned their rehearsal into their content, which collapses the “what do we post” problem and the “when do we rehearse” problem into one problem.
