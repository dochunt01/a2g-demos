The Grammar

Six rules, written for Facebook.

Facebook is where the audience is — 6,900 following Aniba Tha Messenger, against 3,141 on the band's Instagram. That changes the rules, and three of them are the opposite of what works on Reels.

RULE 01 · NOT REELS

Text carries the hook, not sound

Facebook video plays muted in the feed. The winning post won on four lines of on-screen text, not on the vocal.

RULE 02

State a memory, leave a gap

“Back in the day, when music felt like this” works because the viewer fills in the memory. “Live at Haven, Aug 21” gives them nothing to supply.

RULE 03

Name one artist. Never a genre

“Give me Erykah” beat “summer is here” by 56%. A name is a person people have a position about. A genre isn't.

RULE 04 · NOT REELS

Long captions are fine here

This audience reads. Facebook rewards dwell time on the caption. The opposite of Instagram, where long copy gets scrolled past.

RULE 05 · NOT REELS

The ask is a comment, not a link

Facebook suppresses posts that send people away and rewards ones that start conversations. Never end on “link in bio.”

RULE 06

Same frame, every time

One camera position, one look, one overlay style per series. People should recognize Aniba before they read a word.

The One Move

Worth more than every hook on this page.

“Comment TAKE ME BACK and I'll send you the link.”

Not a link in the post. A comment prompt, answered by DM. It costs nothing and it's the only thing here that still exists on August 22nd.

  • 01Comments are what Facebook rewards. This turns the ask into the algorithm's fuel instead of fighting it.
  • 02It builds a list Aniba owns. The venue has the ticket buyers. She'd have every person who raised their hand — in her own inbox, for the next show and the one after.
  • 03It's the only way to count anything. When you don't own the checkout, comments and DMs sent are the number you've got. Crude beats nothing.
  • 04Every commenter is a warm invite. A personal reply converts far better than a public post ever will.

After August

When the covers are behind you.

This whole plan is built for a tribute night. Aniba's own music needs a different engine — six series that compound instead of counting down.

The Session — film the room properly

The highest-leverage change available. Every independent artist who broke through in the last decade turned their rehearsal into their content, which collapses the “what do we post” problem and the “when do we rehearse” problem into one problem.

Cold open mid-take. Cut on the downbeat. The room going quiet after a take lands — that silence is the post.

One Part — twenty seconds on a single detail

Solo the bass line, then the full track. Stack the harmonies one at a time. “This part was a mistake — we kept it.” Craft content travels far outside the fanbase, because other musicians share it.

Aniba Says — stance content

She talks to camera, but each one earns it by having a real position. “The most overrated thing in R&B right now is ___.” “Nobody tells you being a singer is mostly ___.” Her personal page is already the right surface for this.

Chicago — the series that sells tickets

Geo-anchored identity recruits people who can physically show up. Play the song in the place it's about. Rank the local rooms, with opinions. “Chicago artists who should be huge” — name three, tag them, they repost.

The Ask — engineered for comments

“Give me a word, I'll write a hook out of it” — then actually do it, on camera, the next day. Every answer in the comments is a future post you didn't have to think of.

One structural note, worth raising before the next show

The venue owns this event's ticketing, which means it also owns the buyer list, the emails, and every piece of data about who came. The band's promo drives all of it to somebody else's records.

Nothing to fix in eleven days — the DM mechanic is the workaround. But it's worth deciding before the next booking, because it's the same question as owning your masters, just wearing different clothes.

And one small thing: pick a name and hold it

Out in the world right now there's Aniba Tha Messenger, Aniba & The Sol Starz, Aniba Hotep & The Sol Collective, and anibahoteptsc.com. Someone who hears her once and searches a week later has to guess right. Not urgent — just costly, quietly, forever.

One last thing, honestly.

Only two of Aniba's posts were actually measured here — the two in the screenshots. So “name an artist and they'll argue” is a strong-looking signal that happens to match the show's own premise, but it's built on two data points, not a proven pattern.

Treat the eight-day run as the test of it. If the artist-name posts hold above 3% while the vibe posts sit near 2%, it's real and it becomes the house style. If they don't, we learned it in eleven days for free.
