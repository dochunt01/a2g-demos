Playbook

How each post gets made between now and Friday, August 21, 2026. The hub always shows today's — this is the whole run.
The shot

Open on Aniba singing straight into the camera — close, well lit, vertical — for 3 to 5 seconds. Then cut to the stage footage of the same song.

ElementSpec
FormatVertical 9:16, posted as a Reel, on Aniba's personal profile
Length15–25 seconds — the 15-second post out-engaged the 47-second one
ContentThe recognisable part of the song — the hook everyone knows, never the intro
First frameAlready singing. No countdown, no "hey y'all," no title card
CameraOne fixed position, same spot every day — people should recognise her before reading
On-screen textOne line, big, top third — Facebook plays muted, the text is the hook
EndingCut on a note, not a fade, so it loops
Shoot the remaining clips in one sitting. One rehearsal, all the songs, then schedule them out. Filming something new every evening is how a countdown dies on day three. Check the footage from the August 9, 2026 show first — clips that already exist shorten the shoot.
Why her face and not the legends'. A photo of Erykah sells Erykah — and her photos and records are someone else's property, which Facebook's rights-matching can mute. Aniba singing the song is her own footage: the safe choice and the strong one. Writing the artist's name on screen and in the caption is fine — that's just saying what's being performed, and it's the searchable keyword.
The test we're running

Three ways to make the same post. The lanes are pre-assigned below so every day is a controlled comparison — change the shot, keep the length, the page, and the caption structure identical.

Read the score off the public Reels grid: facebook.com/aniba.hotep/reels prints the view count on every thumbnail. If lane B clears 1,000 while lane A sits near 550, that's the house format settled by evidence.
